# Continuous admission: local candidate, not activated

Lester rejected the proposed 100-settlement-attempt/24-hour policy. Neither that proposal nor a replacement monetary allowance is accepted or active. The live service remains v0.8.5 with paid admission disabled. This candidate removes the arbitrary count and expiry while retaining finite, explicit authority and resource checks.

## Implemented

- Production has `maxAttempts:null`, `maxRecords:null` and no automatic `notAfter`. Counters remain durable accounting evidence. Other fixture/test profiles preserve their original finite limits.
- New production settings require an explicit provider budget: integer micro-US-dollars for total authority and a conservative per-attempt reservation. There is no default budget, free-tier assumption, automatic renewal, credit from expected revenue, or automatic price/tax determination. Production rejects a reserve below the current published nonfree $0.001 unit price. The operator must approve an adequate reserve including tax/price exposure before activation; the software cannot guarantee the provider invoice. Unknown or failed attempts retain their reservation. Repeated recovery adds none.
- SQLite's existing atomic reservation increments accounting before settlement. Both configured dollars and stored limits bind to journal identity; restart, changed configuration and a second process cannot reset or overspend them. Changing financial authority later needs a separately reviewed state-preserving procedure; creating a fresh journal to replenish it is prohibited.
- Existing service limits remain: four active operations, 60 requests/minute, 30 verification admissions/minute, eight unknown payments, two outstanding admissions per payer, and an explicitly selected 8–16 whole-MiB reserved order allowance (8 MiB remains the default). Existing disk/WAL and strict proof checks are unchanged. Completed payment records are not pruned automatically.
- Hourly consistent snapshots can retain the two newest verified copies, with a third temporary copy and a 256 MiB total allowance. An older redundant pair is removed only after a fresh quarantined snapshot commits and its own identity/hash is verified. Original order data is never deleted. Interrupted publication/deletion, unexpected files or corruption block further admission pending reconciliation. Rotation is a proposed operational policy, not permission to delete any existing host file. Copies still share the same disk and cannot survive total disk loss.
- Updating source changes the receipt signer's release binding. A narrowly scoped, hash-pinned public grant permits the exact new release to reuse the unchanged original signer. Its original intent, key and installation receipt remain intact. Validation precedes key access. The grant filename is `release-<new transition hash without 0x>.json`, within the immutable held-directory filename limit. No new signer is required.

## Capacity and economics: evidence versus assumptions

The prior v0.8.5 host qualification ran 170 synthetic tests on Node24.22.2 in 78.942 seconds, peaking at 417.59 MiB under the 448 MiB gate. This is functional/resource qualification, not a sustained USDC payment throughput benchmark. Historical local M3 Max results for an older testnet service (about 48–54 mock workflows/second) do not qualify this smaller Render host or finality-bound USDC service. No million-transaction capacity or adoption has been established.

For one small synthetic current job (172 input bytes), measured application response sizes are 1,703 bytes for a quote and 1,368 bytes each for delivery/recovery. Its journal reservation is 19,162 bytes: an 8 MiB allowance holds at most 437 retained jobs of that shape; 16 MiB gives about 875 by the same arithmetic, pending full-state qualification. Larger inputs exhaust it sooner. Reserving one million such jobs would require roughly 19.2 GB before database overhead and backup copies, far beyond the current 1 GB disk and configured limits. Retention/archival and reconciliation work, followed by measured capacity qualification, are required before claiming that scale; silently deleting paid receipts is not an acceptable solution.

One million jobs per 30 days averages 23.15 jobs/minute. Quote+payment+one recovery would average 69.44 service requests/minute, above the current 60-request/minute setting. If all eight pending slots remained occupied for 20 minutes, that alone would limit throughput to about 576 new payments/day. That is an illustrative queue bound, not a measured finality duration or actual throughput promise. Clients must reconcile completed payments to release pending slots. The limit should be raised only after evidence on memory, proof-RPC capacity and durable backlog handling.

For the measured tiny response shape, one million quote+delivery+recovery responses total 4.439 GB of application JSON. This excludes HTTP/TLS overhead, manifests, polling, failures and larger jobs. It is a bandwidth calculation, not a hosting-capacity guarantee.

[Current CDP pricing](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/seller/facilitator) lists the first 1,000 monthly facilitator transactions free and subsequent transactions at $0.001. Exact EVM payments use one onchain settlement each. One million such settlements in one month would therefore imply about $999 in facilitator charges before tax, assuming that pricing/free tier applies to this account and no other usage consumes it. At the still-proposed 0.01-USDC job price, gross receipts would be 10,000 USDC. This is not profit, demand, guaranteed USD value or a permission to incur those charges. Hosting, tax, refunds, operational costs and other provider usage remain separate. Facilitator gas is included in the documented service; it is not paid by the EIP-3009 payer.

## Configurable state allowance

The prior 32-MiB candidate failed actual-host full-state backup verification at the unchanged 120-second limit (1,750 small rows; peak226.89MiB). The maximum is reduced to16MiB; no resource ceiling is relaxed. Preserve that failed fixture and evidence. This release must independently qualify the smaller full state before recommendation.

`productionDraft` accepts `storageBudgetBytes` in whole-MiB increments from 8 through 16 MiB. Omitting it preserves the previous 8 MiB default. The exact allowance is included in the settings hash and durable journal identity. Changing it on an existing journal fails closed; this is initial configuration, not a budget reset or migration procedure. Completed receipts, original payment authorizations and provider accounting are retained. No state archival/deletion, shorter retention, disk expansion, provider charge or activation is implied. The 16 MiB proposal must pass full-state qualification; see the external evidence.

The offline `qualification/state-capacity.mjs` runner fills public synthetic retained rows to the selected bound, backs up the full state, restores it quarantined, recovers first/middle/last receipts across two restarts without provider/signer calls, and verifies new admission fails at the bound. It deliberately uses invented transaction hashes with public fixture signatures: this qualifies storage and recovery, not chain settlement. Resource qualification runs each phase within unchanged 448 MiB and 120-second limits. Tiny and near-maximum-input shapes are separate fixtures.

## Qualification and next necessary actions

All testing uses public synthetic fixtures, not live credentials or provider calls. See bundled evidence for final counts and independent review. Exact Node24.22.2 Linux/native qualification of this changed candidate is required before a release; earlier v0.8.5 results do not qualify it.

The release needs one reviewed, approved source transition plus an explicit receipt grant, reusing the installed Q/CDP/receipt material. Grant fields: `format:mote-receipt-release-v1`, original public `intentHash`, unchanged `receiptSigner`, and exact new `releaseBinding`; pin its canonical hash in `MOTE_RECEIPT_RELEASE_HASH`. Preserve the original source authorization as evidence. Reconcile existing public files before any exclusive creation. Never rewrite the original receipt intent or regenerate its key to make a binding pass.

Activation still requires a confirmed receiving-wallet recovery path, approved price/disclosures/provider allowance and snapshot retention, source/host qualification, exact settings hash, one-time empty production journal initialization and successful admission startup. No active production journal exists in the recorded v0.8.5 handoff; recheck rather than assume. This candidate does not grant authority to deploy, read credentials, activate, accept legal agreements or make a real payment.

The human smoke-test gap is unchanged: the package provides a deterministic EOA signer interface and durable journal, not a ready-to-click wallet checkout. A receiving smart account is supported as a destination but not as the EIP-3009 payer. A verified user-controlled EOA client must retain the exact signed request; the user personally approves any real-value signature/payment. No fixture key, test payer or service signer may be funded for that purpose.
