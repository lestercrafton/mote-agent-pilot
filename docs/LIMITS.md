# Admission, recovery and accounting boundaries

Unpaid quotes are stateless. Local ECDSA request binding is required before a provider call. The standalone HTTP adapter limits connections to32, total headers to24KiB, payment header to22,000 bytes, body to64KiB and request/header timeouts to10s. The combined listener limits connections to32 and retains the legacy16KiB total-header limit,7s request and5s header deadlines; its advertised payment-header limit is12,000 bytes. Headers must also fit the total limit. In both modes, the service allows8 active operations and120 requests/minute per process. These are protective bounds, not a distributed denial-of-service defense or measured hosting capacity.

The shared database additionally enforces120 verification calls/minute,128 short-lived verification records,4 per payer, and30-second verification leases. Invalid/unsupported signatures never reach the provider. A verify failure holds its short lease; retrying after it expires is safe because verification is read-only. These limits are shared across processes and survive restart. Sybil identities can still consume bounded capacity.

Before settlement, one atomic transaction reserves a pending slot, the exact authorization and prepared result, an operator settlement-attempt slot, and bounded result storage. Defaults:32 unknown payments,4 per payer,100 lifetime settlement attempts in the explicitly named budget epoch,10,000 durable order records and64MiB logical result/payment/receipt reservation. Budget epochs cannot silently roll over or be changed on reopen. Changing a budget requires a reviewed migration, not deleting the journal.

An attempt counts conservatively even if no request left the process, the provider rejected it, or its response was lost. This sacrifices availability to prevent an uncertain payment being submitted again. Already reserved orders bypass admission caps for recovery. After an expired authorization is proved unused at a finalized block, its order may be pruned; verification records expire with the authorization. The attempt remains counted. Completed paid receipts remain durable until an explicit export/accounting/retention policy is approved; the record/storage cap fails closed instead of silently discarding recovery data. No legal retention period is asserted.

The64MiB budget bounds logical payload reservations, not SQLite/page/WAL/backup overhead or filesystem usage by other applications. New admission and pre-settlement checks now fail closed below64MiB filesystem free space, above32MiB WAL or above128MiB database size. WAL autocheckpoint is256 pages; SQLite-consistent snapshots and persistent quarantine are implemented. These are sampled watermarks, not disk-space reservations or protection against other processes filling the shared disk. A monitored checkpoint/backup schedule and an approved off-host backup destination remain operational gates. A full/damaged disk causes errors and cannot authorize a new settlement. The proposed existing1GiB host has not been requalified for this adapter.

Caller data is currently restricted to public synthetic/nonsecret inputs. Signed authorizations are bearer capabilities: state files are0600 in a0700 directory, payloads are never logged, and recovery requires the original signed body. Storage encryption, backup destination, privacy disclosures, operator retention and access controls remain production gates.

Client total authority is separately pinned to its journal and policy. It reserves before either signature and holds unknown signing intent. It signs only an exact EIP-3009 amount plus Mote's request binding. No native-gas transaction is exposed. The service/provider is still trusted not to execute a valid authorization outside intended service delivery; the custom binding is enforced by Mote, not by USDC's contract. The payer must preauthorize the origin and receiving address.

Facilitator billing is offchain. The code caps settlement calls, **not a provider invoice under arbitrary future pricing or activity elsewhere in the project**. At the researched$0.001 rate,100 attempts imply at most$0.10 of published per-transaction fees if every attempt were billable, before any applicable free tier and subject to provider terms. No paid usage is authorized or configured here. Do not mistake the free tier for a provider-enforced spending cap. Fixture price1,000 base units =0.001USDC would leave no margin over that per-settlement fee; it is not an approved commercial price.
